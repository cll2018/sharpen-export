    <p>Steel-bonded cemented carbide targets tooling and harsh wear-resistant components — in the annealed state it can be directly machined by turning, milling, and drilling; it supports forging and welding, and complex forming can be completed without special equipment. After quench hardening, the hardness can reach HRC 60–70, wear resistance is close to that of high-cobalt cemented carbide, toughness is far superior to ordinary cemented carbide, and impact resistance is stronger. By adjusting the steel of the binder phase, special properties such as wear resistance, corrosion resistance, and heat resistance can be obtained; full lifecycle cost is far lower than conventional alloy die steel, die life can be increased by a dozen to a few dozen times, significantly reducing mold change downtime.</p>
    <h2>Technical Parameters</h2>
      <ul class="specs">
      <li>Applications: Cold extrusion, cold upsetting, and blanking/punching dies; refractory brick forming dies; replaces YG20 type cemented carbide for use in high-load die cores.</li>
      <li>Cutting tools: Cemented carbide and high-speed steel tools used for titanium alloys and nickel alloys, rock coring, and geological drilling.</li>
      <li>Wear-resistant components: Rollers, nozzles, bearings; higher grade grades are used for aerospace and submarine navigation components.</li>
      <li>Diamond/CBN grinding wheel fixtures and CBN milling forming fixtures support; meets precision and wear requirements.</li>
