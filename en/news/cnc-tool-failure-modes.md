<p>*Release date: 2016-11-07* In the cutting process, when the tool wears to a certain limit, the cutting edge chips or breaks, or the cutting edge rolls (plastic deformation), the tool loses its cutting ability or cannot guarantee processing quality, which is called tool failure. The main forms of tool damage, causes and countermeasures are as follows: 1. Flank wear: Friction wear appearing on the flank face caused by mechanical stress. Tool materials with high wear resistance should be selected, and cutting speed reduced, feed rate increased, and the tool relief angle enlarged. 2. Edge wear: Edge wear on the major cutting edge is common at the contact surface with the workpiece. Cutting speed and feed speed should be reduced, and tool materials with high wear resistance should be selected and the rake angle enlarged to make the cutting edge sharp. 3. Rake face wear (crater wear): Wear on the rake face caused by friction and diffusion. The main measure is to reduce cutting speed and feed speed, and select coated cemented carbide material. 4. Plastic deformation: Deformation of the cutting edge caused by high temperature or high stress. Measures should be taken to reduce cutting speed and feed speed, and select tool materials with high wear resistance and high thermal conductivity. 5. Built-up edge: Adhesion of workpiece material to the tool. Countermeasures include increasing cutting speed, selecting tool materials with low affinity such as coated cemented carbide or cermet, and using coolant. 6. Edge peeling: Some very small notches appear on the cutting edge, rather than uniform wear. Feed speed should be reduced at the start of machining, and tool materials with good toughness and inserts with high cutting edge strength should be selected.</p>
